# Combat Encounters

In AI-GM, combat is dynamic, tactical, and unpredictable. Enemies think for themselves using real AI—not just "roll attack, roll damage." This section explains how combat works and what to expect.

## How Combat Starts

Combat begins when:
- You encounter a hostile creature or enemy
- An NPC attacks you
- You initiate an attack on something dangerous
- A scripted encounter triggers

Once combat starts, the AI-GM:
1. Rolls initiative for all combatants
2. Takes control of enemy actions and tactics
3. Handles turn-by-turn progression
4. Makes real-time decisions about strategy

You still control your character(s)—the AI only controls the enemies.

## Enemy Tactics

Unlike traditional D&D where enemies follow preset patterns, AI-GM enemies **think tactically**:

- **Self-preservation** — Enemies retreat if badly wounded or outnumbered
- **Targeting logic** — They attack healers and dangerous enemies first
- **Positioning** — They use terrain, cover, and distance strategically
- **Resources** — They use special abilities and spells when it matters
- **Morale** — They flee, surrender, or fight harder based on circumstances
- **Teamwork** — Multiple enemies coordinate attacks and flanking

This means combat is unpredictable. An enemy might flee when you expect a last stand, or double down when you think they're beaten.

## Your Turn in Combat

When it's your turn:
1. Describe what your character does (attack, spell, movement, etc.)
2. Make any rolls needed (attack rolls, saving throws, etc.)
3. Apply results (damage, status effects, etc.)
4. End your turn

The AI-GM will resolve your action's effects and narrate the consequences.

### Common Actions

| Action | How it Works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Attack** | Roll to hit. On success, roll damage. AI-GM tracks enemy health. |
| **Cast Spell** | Roll attack or ask enemies for saving throws as needed. |
| **Use Item** | Activate potions, scrolls, or magic items. Describe the effect. |
| **Dodge/Disengage** | Declare it. Enemies get disadvantage on attacks (or skip their turn to move). |
| **Heal** | Cast healing spells or use potions. AI-GM tracks HP for allies. |
| **Move** | Describe where you go. Movement is continuous, not grid-based. |

## Health & Damage

AI-GM tracks enemy health automatically. When you hit an enemy:
- Describe the attack
- Roll damage
- Tell the AI-GM how much
- The AI-GM updates the enemy's health

You'll be told when enemies are wounded, badly hurt, or near death.

### Healing

You can heal yourself and allies with spells, potions, or class abilities. Tell the AI-GM "I cast *Cure Wounds*" or "I drink a healing potion" and describe where you target it.

## Special Situations

### Multiple Enemies

When fighting several enemies, the AI-GM manages all of them fairly. Each takes their turn and acts according to their tactics. You control your character(s) and decide how to handle the threats.

### Allies in Combat

Other characters in your party (NPCs or other players) act when it's their turn. They follow their own tactics:
- Player characters follow player decisions
- NPC allies follow AI decisions (supporting the party)
- Enemies act against everyone

### Environmental Hazards

Combat might include fire, collapsing buildings, poisoned air, or other dangers. The AI-GM will tell you about these and you can use them tactically (pushing enemies into fire, hiding from lightning, etc.).

### Retreat & Surrender

If you want to retreat or surrender, tell the AI-GM:
- "We're falling back to the bridge"
- "I drop my weapon and surrender"
- "I cast *Dimension Door* and disappear"

The AI-GM will narrate the consequences. Enemies might let you go, pursue, or demand your surrender.

## Combat Ends

Combat ends when:
- All enemies are defeated
- You defeat the enemies and take their loot
- You retreat and escape
- You surrender (and negotiations follow)
- A natural conclusion happens (the enemy leader surrenders, talks break out, etc.)

After combat, you have time to:
- Heal up and rest
- Collect loot and treasure
- Talk to survivors
- Return to exploration

## Tips for Great Combat

**Before combat:**
- Talk to the AI-GM about any fears (TPK, etc.) and trust the story
- Have your character sheet ready
- Know what spells/abilities you want to use

**During combat:**
- Be descriptive—"I swing my sword" becomes more exciting as "I aim for the creature's sword arm"
- Ask questions about the battlefield ("Can I see the mage from here?")
- Use the environment (high ground, cover, narrow spaces)
- Coordinate with your party

**After combat:**
- Take time to recover and roleplay the aftermath
- Loot treasure and discuss what it means
- Plan your next move

## Difficulty Levels

AI-GM adjusts combat difficulty based on:
- Your party's level and equipment
- Number of enemies
- Enemy tactics and types
- Campaign tone (deadly, challenging, moderate)

If combat is too easy, trust that the story has a reason. If it's too hard and you want easier combat, ask the AI-GM to adjust.

## Troubleshooting

**"An enemy did something I didn't expect"**
→ AI enemies make real decisions. This is a feature! Combat is more dynamic and less predictable.

**"A character died"**
→ Death is possible in AI-GM, but usually rare. Decide up front whether death is a real risk at your table; if a specific death lands badly, pause the AI and correct it in Foundry — the audit trail shows the exact hit point changes.

**"Combat is taking too long"**
→ Ask the AI-GM to speed up enemy turns or narrate multiple rounds at once.

**"I don't understand what's happening"**
→ Ask the AI-GM to describe the scene clearly. Say "What do I see right now?"
