# Semantic Lore System

The Semantic Lore System is what makes AI-GM feel coherent and responsive. It's an advanced AI system that remembers and understands everything about your campaign—characters, relationships, history, secrets, and context. This means the world feels alive, consistent, and deeply connected.

## How It Works

### What the Lore System Remembers

The system tracks:

**Character Details:**
- Names, appearances, personalities
- Backstories and goals
- Skills and abilities
- Relationships with other characters
- Secrets and vulnerabilities

**Settlement Information:**
- History and origin
- Current political structure
- Economic conditions
- Cultural values
- Population and key locations

**Story Elements:**
- Quest chains and outcomes
- Major conflicts and alliances
- Prophecies and legends
- Supernatural phenomena
- Hidden secrets

**Relationships:**
- Who knows whom
- Trust and suspicion
- Romantic connections
- Business partnerships
- Feuds and rivalries

**Consequences:**
- Past actions and their effects
- Promises made and broken
- Debts owed and favors given
- Reputations earned
- Patterns of player behavior

### Semantic Understanding

Unlike a simple database, the Lore System **understands context**:

- It knows that "the merchant who was robbed" and "Lord Thayer" are the same person
- It recognizes that saving someone's life creates debt
- It understands that a wizard's tower and a mage's sanctum are related
- It infers that breaking a promise damages trust

This semantic depth creates natural, coherent responses from NPCs and the world.

## How It Improves Your Campaign

### NPCs Feel Consistent

When you talk to an NPC:
- They reference past events you've done together
- They remember what you've discussed
- They hold grudges or show gratitude appropriately
- They don't contradict themselves
- Their personality remains consistent

**Example:**
You help a tavern keeper defeat rival merchants. Months later, you return.

Without Lore System: "What brings you to my tavern?"
With Lore System: "The hero returns! You'll always have a place here. Those rival merchants tried again last month, but your reputation scared them off."

### The World Feels Connected

Story elements link naturally:

- An NPC's personal quest connects to a larger conspiracy
- A settlement's history explains current conflicts
- Legends mention past adventures
- Quests build on each other
- Unrelated events turn out to have connections

**Example:**
Early in the campaign, you hear a legend about a lost temple. Months later, a quest-giver mentions their family once lived near an old ruin. The Lore System recognizes the connection and weaves them together.

### NPCs Make Smart Decisions

Because the system understands context, NPCs:
- Give information that's actually useful
- Suggest quests aligned with your capabilities
- React appropriately to your reputation
- Avoid obvious traps you'd see through
- Plan coherently with their allies

### Surprises Feel Earned

Plot twists that work well are grounded in established lore:

- A villain is revealed to be someone you knew
- A quest hook connects to a past event
- A prophecy turns out to be true
- A seemingly random NPC becomes important

These surprises satisfy because they feel logical in retrospect.

## The Vault

The "Vault" is your campaign's lore repository. It includes everything the system knows, organized for clarity.

### What's in the Vault

**Character Profiles:**
```
Name: Marta Crane
Title: Tavern Keeper
Personality: Adventurous, protective
History: Former pirate, settled down 15 years ago
Relationships: Married to the blacksmith, protective of her niece
Secret: She knows where pirate treasure is buried
Current Status: Runs the best tavern in Port Redhold
```

**Settlement Records:**
```
Name: Port Redhold
Type: Coastal trading city
Population: ~8,000
History: Founded as a pirate haven, now legitimate trade center
Political Leader: The Merchant Council
Key Locations: The Red Anchor (tavern), the Grand Market, the Temple of Tides
Current Issues: Rival merchant families competing for dominance
```

**Quest Logs:**
```
Quest: Find the Missing Heir
Quest-giver: Lord Thayer
Status: In Progress
Progress: You found evidence in the temple
Rewards: 500 gold, Lord's favor
Consequences: Lord Thayer now owes you
```

**Lore Entries:**
```
Legend: The Sunken Gold
Description: Ancient pirate treasure lies beneath the harbor
Mentions: Marta Crane, Old Pirate Maps
Relevance: Possible motivation for mysterious events
```

### Using the Vault

You can:
- **Read entries** to refresh your memory
- **Add notes** to mark important information
- **Link entries** to show connections
- **Search** for specific information
- **Export** for personal records

The Lore System uses the Vault to inform NPC behavior, generate quests, and create connections.

## Advanced Lore Features

### Thematic Coherence

The system identifies themes in your campaign and reinforces them:

- **If redemption is a theme:** Quests involve characters finding forgiveness
- **If tragedy is a theme:** NPCs face difficult choices with high stakes
- **If hope is a theme:** Even dark moments contain light

Themes emerge naturally, making the campaign feel like a unified story.

### Dynamic Lore

The Vault updates as you play:
- Completing a quest updates quest status
- Learning a secret updates character profiles
- Defeating an enemy updates their status
- Changing relationships updates NPC records

This keeps the Vault current and accurate.

### Conflicting Information

Sometimes you learn contradictory information (two NPCs tell different stories, or an NPC seems to contradict themselves). The Lore System handles this:

- It recognizes when NPCs might be lying
- It notes alternative interpretations
- It creates mystery and investigation hooks
- It allows for revelation and surprise

This creates depth. Not everything is what it seems.

## Lore Injection

The system automatically weaves lore into your world:

### In NPC Dialogue

NPCs reference past events and shared history naturally:

"Last time we spoke, you mentioned fearing the dark. That's why I thought of you for this request."

### In Environmental Details

Locations reflect their history:
- A wealthy merchant's home shows signs of success
- A haunted location shows signs of tragedy
- A temple reflects its deity's values

### In Quest Design

Quests are built from lore:
- NPCs offer quests aligned with their goals
- Quests reference past events
- Quests create new story threads

### In Combat

Enemies reference history:
- "You killed my brother!"
- "I've heard stories about your power"
- "Let's settle this once and for all"

## Best Practices

### Feed the Lore System

The more you tell the system, the better it works:

**Do:**
- Tell the AI-GM important character details
- Explain your party's goals
- Describe relationships that matter
- Reveal secrets when appropriate
- Ask about lore and history

**Don't:**
- Keep important information hidden
- Change major facts without telling the system
- Assume the system knows context it hasn't been told
- Ignore lore contradictions (mention them)

### Reference Lore

When you reference past events, the system learns:

"That's the temple where we found the map earlier."
"I remember she mentioned her daughter was sick."
"This is where the bandits ambushed us."

The system adds these references to the Vault.

### Create Lore Deliberately

Plan aspects of your campaign's lore:

- Define key relationships
- Establish settlement history
- Create mysteries to uncover
- Plant secrets to reveal
- Build conspiracies to uncover

Then let the system fill in details naturally.

## Example: Lore in Action

### Setup
Early in a campaign, you help a scholar escape from assassins. She's researching an ancient prophecy.

### Lore Created
- Scholar: "Helps players, is researching prophecies"
- Prophecy: "Mentioned by scholar"
- Assassins: "Hunting the scholar"
- Relationship: "Scholar trusts the party"

### Weeks Later
You return to a settlement. A noble offers you a quest to steal the prophecy from the scholar.

The Lore System generates:
- The noble doesn't know the scholar is your ally
- The noble wants the prophecy for rival reasons
- This creates a moral choice (disappoint the noble or betray the scholar)
- The quest feels natural, not random

### Resolution
However you decide, the Lore System updates:
- If you help the scholar: She offers a major reward, the noble becomes an enemy
- If you betray her: She becomes hostile, the noble becomes an ally (for now)
- If you negotiate: Both benefit, complicated relationship forms

All of this emerges from the Lore System understanding context.

## Integration with Other Systems

### Living World
The Lore System tracks how the world changes. As time passes, NPC goals are updated in the Vault.

### Combat
Enemies reference lore during combat, making fights feel personal.

### Settlement Growth
As settlements change, the Vault is updated to reflect new NPCs, political shifts, and physical changes.

### Campaign Generation
The system uses lore seeds to generate cohesive campaigns.
