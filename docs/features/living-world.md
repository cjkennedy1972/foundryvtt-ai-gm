# Living World System

The most distinctive feature of AI-GM is that the world lives between sessions. NPCs don't wait frozen until you return—they age, move, form relationships, and respond to consequences. This creates a genuinely alive world that reacts to your actions.

## How the Living World Works

### Time Passes

When you end a session, time advances in the world. You control how much:

- **One day passes** — Quick turnaround, urgent situations continue
- **One week passes** — Standard interval between sessions
- **One month passes** — Significant time for major changes
- **One season/year passes** — Large-scale changes accumulate

As time passes:

**NPCs act:** They follow their goals and routines
**Relationships form:** NPCs get closer or more distant
**Conflicts escalate:** Disputes deepen or resolve
**Settlements change:** Buildings appear/disappear, populations shift
**The world evolves:** Consequences accumulate

### NPC Autonomy

Between sessions, NPCs aren't idle. They:

**Work their jobs:**
- Merchants expand or fail their businesses
- Guards patrol and respond to crime
- Craftspeople create and trade
- Nobles hold court and make alliances

**Pursue personal goals:**
- A merchant dreams of opening a second shop
- A warrior trains to become a master
- A scholar researches ancient secrets
- A bard builds their reputation

**Form relationships:**
- NPCs fall in love
- Friendships deepen
- Rivalries intensify
- Alliances form

**React to the world:**
- If you helped a family, they prosper
- If you angered the guard, they watch you
- If you revealed a secret, it spreads
- If you left a problem unsolved, it worsens

### Consequences Stack

What you do has weight:

**Positive:**
- Help an NPC → They become loyal
- Solve a problem → The settlement improves
- Give information → It spreads and helps others
- Build relationships → NPCs aid you later

**Negative:**
- Steal from someone → They hold a grudge
- Cause harm → Enemies form or grow stronger
- Make promises → Breaking them damages trust
- Leave problems → They grow worse

**Unpredictable:**
- Your actions have ripple effects
- A favor to one NPC might anger another
- Solving one problem creates new ones
- Alliances shift as circumstances change

### Emergent Stories

The most rewarding part: stories emerge organically from the living world.

**Example 1:**
You help a farmgirl escape an arranged marriage to a merchant. Months pass. When you return, the girl has started a new life with a blacksmith—they're happy, have a child, and the merchant has become bitter. The merchant is now a quest-giver with a grudge.

**Example 2:**
You defeat bandits threatening a village. Months pass. The village prospers without the threat. A charismatic NPC recruit rises to leadership. When you return, that NPC is now the mayor and treats you like old friends.

**Example 3:**
You ignore a mysterious disappearance. Months pass. Similar disappearances happen. When you return, the settlement is frightened, the town guard is overwhelmed, and the mysterious force has grown.

These stories aren't scripted. They emerge from the world responding to your choices.

## Settlement Growth & Change

### What Changes

**Physical:**
- New buildings appear (taverns, shops, temples)
- Damaged areas rebuild or crumble
- Population grows or shrinks
- Districts change character

**Political:**
- New leaders rise to power
- Alliances shift
- Factions form or dissolve
- External threats emerge

**Economic:**
- Businesses succeed or fail
- Trade routes change
- Wealth increases or decreases
- New industries develop

**Social:**
- Relationships between NPCs evolve
- Families form and break
- Social hierarchy changes
- Conflicts escalate or resolve

### Control the Pace

You decide how much change happens:

**Stable world:** Little changes between sessions; focus on character stories
**Dynamic world:** Significant changes; the world evolves visibly
**Chaotic world:** Rapid transformation; visiting old locations reveals massive change

This is a campaign setting, not a constraint. Adjust as you play.

## NPC Development

### Long-term Arcs

NPCs have arcs that play out over the campaign:

**Redemption Arc:**
A criminal works toward becoming respectable. Over months, they change. By the time you return, they might have become a trusted community member.

**Power Arc:**
A nobody dreams of leading. If you help them, they rise. If you oppose them, they might become a villain.

**Love Arc:**
Two NPCs navigate a relationship. Help them along, and they marry. Interfere, and they part bitterly.

**Tragedy Arc:**
An NPC faces hardship. Without intervention, they fall. With your help, they recover.

These arcs play out whether you're watching or not. Returning to a settlement reveals how stories have progressed.

### Relationships Change

NPCs remember you:

**If you helped them:**
- They greet you warmly
- They offer better deals and quests
- They support you in danger
- They reference past events

**If you betrayed them:**
- They're cool or hostile
- They refuse services
- They might become enemies
- They spread negative rumors

**If you haven't seen them:**
- They ask about your adventures
- They share what they've been doing
- They've changed (age, new skills, different status)

Relationships feel real because they have history.

## Seasonal Cycles

If you play over "seasons," the world reflects this:

**Spring:**
- Growth and renewal
- Festivals and celebrations
- Travel resumes
- New businesses open

**Summer:**
- Peak harvest
- Maximum commerce
- Wars and violence increase
- Long, bright days

**Autumn:**
- Harvest completed
- Preparation for winter
- Migration and travel
- Shorter days

**Winter:**
- Scarcity and hardship
- Isolation
- Death increases
- Magical events might increase

Settlements change with seasons. A coastal town bustles in summer but quiets in winter. A farming village is wealthy after harvest but poor by spring.

## World-Scale Events

Major events can happen without you:

**Disasters:**
- A plague hits a settlement
- A natural disaster (earthquake, flood)
- A fire destroys a district
- A creature attack decimates the population

**Political:**
- A noble is assassinated
- A new faction takes power
- A war breaks out
- A kingdom falls

**Supernatural:**
- A magical event corrupts the land
- Undead rise
- A ancient evil awakens
- A blessing manifests

When you return, you find the world changed. This creates opportunities for new stories and explores consequences of absence.

## Living World Settings

### Activity Level

**Quiet:** Slow change; NPCs develop gradually
**Normal:** Noticeable change; a few major events
**Active:** Dramatic change; several major events

Higher activity means more happens between sessions. Choose what feels right.

### Player Impact

**Strongly player-driven:** NPCs change mainly based on player actions
**Balanced:** Player actions matter, but the world has its own momentum
**World-driven:** The world evolves with or without player involvement

This affects how central players feel to the world.

### Time Scale

**Fast:** One session = months pass
**Normal:** One session = one week passes
**Slow:** One session = few days pass

This affects how much changes between sessions.

## Tips for a Thriving Living World

**Before playing:**
- Set time scale and activity level expectations
- Discuss world tone (how connected do players feel?)
- Identify major NPCs to track

**During sessions:**
- Pay attention to NPC relationships
- Notice what NPCs care about
- Your actions ripple through the world
- Trust the system

**Between sessions:**
- Ask the AI-GM what happened
- Read the summary of changes
- Anticipate how NPCs have developed
- Plan your return knowing the world changed

**Advanced:**
- Track key relationships across the campaign
- Revisit old locations to see how they've changed
- Build long-term plans knowing time passes
- Create situations where past choices matter

## Examples of Living Worlds

### Example 1: The Riverside Town
You help a merchant clear bandits. They thank you and close their tavern for the season (lives a quiet life). You leave.

Months pass.

You return. The merchant has reopened, expanded, and hired staff. Locals recognize you as the hero who saved the town. A new threat has emerged because the merchant's success attracts envious rivals.

### Example 2: The Noble's Favor
You assist a young noble in a dispute with their father. The noble appreciates you.

Months pass.

You return. The young noble is now the lord (their father retired). They remember you fondly, offer better pay, and trust you with sensitive tasks. The old lord might still hold a grudge.

### Example 3: The Ignored Crisis
You discover evidence of a growing cult but don't investigate further.

Months pass.

You return. The cult has grown. Disappearances have escalated. When you finally investigate, the problem is much bigger and more dangerous.
